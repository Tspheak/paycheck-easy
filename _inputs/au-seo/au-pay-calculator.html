How Australian take-home pay is worked out

Your take-home pay is your gross pay minus income tax, the Medicare levy and, if you have one, a compulsory study loan repayment. Superannuation is usually paid by your employer on top of your salary, so it doesn't reduce your pay unless your package is quoted “including super”.

2026–27 resident tax rates

From 1 July 2026 the rate on income between $18,201 and $45,000 drops from 16% to 15%. The other brackets are unchanged.

The Medicare levy is 2% of taxable income. Low-income earners pay a reduced levy or none; the 2026–27 low-income thresholds are rates to be confirmed (ATO has published 2025–26 figures: $28,011 / $35,013 for singles). Our examples are well above those thresholds, so they're not affected.

The low income tax offset (LITO) of up to $700 still applies for incomes up to $66,667 (ATO).

FAQ

What changed for 2026–27?

The 16% rate on taxable income from $18,201 to $45,000 is now 15%, so the tax at the top of that bracket falls from $4,288 to $4,020. The higher brackets keep the same rates and thresholds.

Does this include super?

Our examples treat super as paid on top of your salary at the 12% super guarantee rate. If your salary package includes super, untick the box and we'll split it out.

Why is my payslip slightly different?

Employers withhold tax using ATO withholding schedules that spread offsets and rounding across the year. Your actual tax is settled when you lodge your return.

Does it work for non-residents or working holiday makers?

This page covers Australian residents who claim the tax-free threshold. Non-resident and working holiday maker rates are different and aren't shown here.
