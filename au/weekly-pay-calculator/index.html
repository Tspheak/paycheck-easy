How weekly pay is calculated

The yearly column annualises your weekly pay (× 52) and applies the 2026–27 resident tax rates and Medicare levy. The weekly column uses ATO Schedule 1 withholding, which already includes Medicare, plus Schedule 8 if you have a HELP debt. That is not the yearly tax divided by 52.

Some years contain 53 paydays for weekly-paid workers. When that happens your employer may withhold slightly more tax each week so the year doesn't end with a shortfall.

The Medicare levy is 2% of taxable income. Low-income earners pay a reduced levy or none. For singles, the 2026–27 low-income thresholds are $28,011 and $35,013.

The low income tax offset (LITO) of up to $700 still applies for incomes up to $66,667 (ATO). The ATO page for LITO is not labelled with an income year.

FAQ

How do I convert an hourly rate to weekly pay?

Multiply your hourly rate by the hours you work in a normal week. For example $40 × 38 hours = $1,520 a week before tax. Choose Hourly in the calculator and enter both figures.

Is super taken out of my weekly pay?

No, not in the usual arrangement. The 12% super guarantee is paid by your employer on top of your wage. From 1 July 2026 it must be paid each payday under Payday Super.

What if my hours change every week?

Enter an average week for a yearly picture, or use the Paycheck Pocket app to log each shift.

Does overtime change my tax rate?

Overtime is taxed as ordinary income. A large week can mean more tax withheld that week, but your final tax is based on your total income for the year.
