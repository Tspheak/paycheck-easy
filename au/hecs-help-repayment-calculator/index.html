How HELP repayments work from 2026–27

Since 2025–26, compulsory repayments are marginal: you repay only on the part of your income above the minimum repayment income, rather than a percentage of your whole income. For 2026–27 that minimum is $69,528.

Repayment income (2026–27)Repayment
$0 – $69,528Nil
$69,529 – $129,71715c for each $1 over $69,528
$129,718 – $186,050$9,028 plus 17c for each $1 over $129,717
$186,051 and over10% of total repayment income

Source: ATO, Study and training loan repayment thresholds and rates. The ATO shows the second-band base as $9,028.

Repayment income is more than your salary. It adds reportable fringe benefits, net investment losses, reportable super contributions and exempt foreign employment income to your taxable income. This estimate assumes repayment income equals the taxable income you enter.

The same thresholds apply to HELP, VET Student Loans, SFSS, Student Start-up Loans, ABSTUDY SSL and Australian Apprenticeship Support Loans.

The Medicare levy is 2% of taxable income. For singles, the 2026–27 low-income thresholds are $28,011 and $35,013. The per-pay HELP column uses ATO Schedule 8, which can differ from the yearly repayment divided by the number of pays.

FAQ

Is HECS the same as HELP?

Yes. HECS-HELP is the HELP loan most university students have. People still call it HECS.

When do I start repaying?

Compulsory repayments start once your 2026–27 repayment income is more than $69,528.

Why is my employer withholding more than this estimate?

Employers withhold for study loans each pay using ATO schedules. Any over- or under-withholding is settled on your notice of assessment.

Can I pay extra?

Yes, you can make voluntary repayments to the ATO at any time. They count on top of your compulsory repayment.
