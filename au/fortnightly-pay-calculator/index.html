Fortnightly pay, explained

Most Australian employers pay fortnightly. The yearly column multiplies your fortnightly gross by 26 and applies the 2026–27 resident tax rates and the Medicare levy. The fortnightly column uses ATO Schedule 1 withholding and Schedule 8 for HELP.

Every 11 years or so there are 27 fortnightly paydays in a financial year. Employers can withhold a little extra tax each fortnight in those years.

The Medicare levy is 2% of taxable income. Low-income earners pay a reduced levy or none. For singles, the 2026–27 low-income thresholds are $28,011 and $35,013.

The low income tax offset (LITO) of up to $700 still applies for incomes up to $66,667 (ATO). The ATO page for LITO is not labelled with an income year.

Rent and bills are often monthly. A fortnightly amount × 26 ÷ 12 gives the monthly equivalent, not × 2.

FAQ

How do I turn an annual salary into fortnightly pay?

Divide the annual figure by 26. A $72,800 salary is $2,800 a fortnight before tax.

Why isn't fortnightly pay just half of monthly?

There are 26 fortnights in a year but 12 months, so a month is about 2.17 fortnights.

Does HELP come out of each fortnightly pay?

If you've told your employer you have a study loan, they withhold extra each pay. See our HECS/HELP repayment page for 2026–27 rates.

Is super included?

No. Super of 12% is paid by your employer on top of your pay, unless your package is quoted including super.
