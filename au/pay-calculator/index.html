How Australian take-home pay is worked out

Your take-home pay is your gross pay minus income tax, the Medicare levy and, if you have one, a compulsory study loan repayment. Superannuation is usually paid by your employer on top of your salary, so it doesn't reduce your pay unless your package is quoted “including super”. The yearly column is that assessment. The per-pay column is the income tax taken out of each pay. It already includes Medicare, and a study-loan amount when you have a HELP debt.

2026–27 resident tax rates

From 1 July 2026 the rate on income between $18,201 and $45,000 drops from 16% to 15%. The other brackets are unchanged.

Source: ATO, Tax rates – Australian residents. Excludes the 2% Medicare levy.

The Medicare levy is 2% of taxable income. Low-income earners pay a reduced levy or none. For singles, the 2026–27 low-income thresholds are $28,011 and $35,013.

The low income tax offset (LITO) of up to $700 still applies for incomes up to $66,667 (ATO). The ATO page for LITO is not labelled with an income year.

FAQ

What changed for 2026–27?

The 16% rate on taxable income from $18,201 to $45,000 is now 15%, so the tax at the top of that bracket falls from $4,288 to $4,020. The higher brackets keep the same rates and thresholds.

Does this include super?

The estimate treats super as paid on top of your salary at the 12% super guarantee rate, unless you untick that box. Unticking it treats the amount as a package that includes super, and splits out cash salary as the package divided by 1.12, up to the maximum contribution base.

Why is my payslip slightly different?

The per-pay column is the income tax taken out, using the same formulas employers use. It is not the yearly tax divided by the number of pays. A tax offset or an extra payday in the year can still change the amount on your payslip. Your assessment is settled when you lodge your return.

Does it work for non-residents or working holiday makers?

This page covers Australian residents who claim the tax-free threshold. Non-resident and working holiday maker rates are different and aren't shown here.
